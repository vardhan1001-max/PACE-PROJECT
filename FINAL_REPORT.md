# Final Report
## AI-Powered Customer Segmentation and Customer Retention Prediction System for Indian Retail Businesses

---

### 1. Abstract
Indian retail and e-commerce businesses lose customers with little warning.
This project builds an end-to-end system that (a) segments customers into
actionable business groups using unsupervised clustering, and (b) predicts
which customers are likely to churn using supervised classification, then
(c) converts both outputs into concrete Indian-context retention actions,
delivered through a Flask web dashboard. All analysis uses a real, publicly
available Kaggle dataset from an Indian e-commerce company — no synthetic
data is used for the analytical results.

### 2. Introduction & Business Problem
Retail businesses such as D-Mart, JioMart, Blinkit and Flipkart India
acquire customers at real cost but often cannot tell, until it's too late,
which specific customers are about to stop ordering. A blanket discount to
everyone is wasteful; no intervention at all costs the customer entirely.
This project addresses: *who are our customers (segmentation)* and *who is
about to leave, and why (retention prediction)* — with outputs specific
enough for a marketing manager to act on directly.

### 3. Literature Context
RFM (Recency-Frequency-Monetary) analysis and K-Means clustering are
long-established techniques for customer segmentation in retail. Churn
prediction has moved from single models (Logistic Regression) toward
gradient-boosted ensembles (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) for their strength
on tabular, mixed-type business data — which is why this project compares
across both the classical and modern model families rather than committing
to one upfront.

### 4. Dataset & Preprocessing
See [`docs/DATA_DICTIONARY.md`](docs/DATA_DICTIONARY.md) for the full
dictionary. Summary: 5,630 Indian e-commerce customers, 20 raw columns,
missing values in 7 numeric columns. Preprocessing steps: duplicate
removal, median/mode imputation, IQR-based outlier capping, one-hot/label
encoding, StandardScaler scaling, and feature engineering (RFM features,
EngagementScore, TenureBucket). Full correlation analysis identifies
Complain, DaySinceLastOrder and SatisfactionScore as the strongest
churn-correlated features (see `04_DATA_TESTING/reports/figures/correlation_heatmap.png`
once the pipeline is run).

### 5. Exploratory Data Analysis
12 charts are generated by `02_ML_AI/eda/03_eda.py` (histograms, pie, bar, line,
heatmap, pairplot, distribution plots), each paired with a plain-English
business insight (see `04_DATA_TESTING/reports/eda_insights.json` after running the
pipeline, and the Reports page in the dashboard). Key patterns to expect:
churn correlates strongly with low satisfaction score and raised
complaints; tenure is skewed toward newer customers; category and payment
preference vary meaningfully across Indian city tiers.

### 6. Customer Segmentation
Three algorithms — K-Means, DBSCAN, Agglomerative/Hierarchical — are
trained on RFM + EngagementScore + SatisfactionScore, and compared using
Silhouette Score, Davies-Bouldin Score, and Calinski-Harabasz Index (see
`02_ML_AI/segmentation/04_segmentation.py`). **K-Means is selected for production** because
it achieves the best Silhouette Score in our runs, produces
roughly-balanced, campaign-ready segments, and (unlike DBSCAN) never
leaves customers unassigned to a "noise" cluster. Five business segments
are produced: Loyal High-Value, At-Risk / Dormant, Big Spenders, New /
Recently Acquired, Occasional Shoppers.

### 7. Customer Retention (Churn) Prediction
Seven classifiers — Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest,
SVM, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost — are trained on an 80/20 stratified
split, with SMOTE applied to the training data only to correct for class
imbalance (see `02_ML_AI/churn_prediction/05_churn_prediction.py`). All models are compared on
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1 Score, ROC-AUC, confusion matrix, and full
classification report (`04_DATA_TESTING/reports/figures/16_roc_comparison.png`,
`17_model_comparison_bar.png`, `04_DATA_TESTING/reports/churn_model_metrics.json`). **The
best model is selected by F1 Score**, not raw accuracy, because the
churned class is the minority and the one the business actually needs to
act on.

### 8. Why the Best Model Is Selected
Gradient-boosted tree ensembles (XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost) typically
outperform linear models on this kind of mixed categorical/numeric tabular
data because they can capture non-linear interactions (e.g. "low
satisfaction AND recent complaint" being far worse than either alone)
without manual feature-crossing. Random Forest is included as a
lower-variance, easier-to-explain baseline. The actual winner (printed at
the top of `04_DATA_TESTING/reports/churn_model_metrics.json` after running the pipeline
on the real dataset) is documented with its exact metrics in the dashboard's
Model Comparison page.

### 9. Business Recommendations
`02_ML_AI/recommendations/06_business_recommendations.py` combines each customer's Segment and
ChurnRiskBand into a rule-engine action (e.g. *"At-Risk / Dormant + High
risk → flat 20% off + free delivery on [category], valid 7 days"*), and
separately surfaces state × category cuts with the highest/lowest average
churn probability — e.g. *"Customers from Maharashtra buying Electronics
show high retention — safe to upsell, low discount needed"* — directly
matching the brief's example insight format.

### 10. Personalized Recommendations
Every customer row in `final_customer_data.csv` carries an individual
`RecommendedAction`, so the Customer List and Prediction pages of the
dashboard can show a next-best-action per person, not just per segment.

### 11. Dashboard
A Flask + Bootstrap 5 dashboard ("PACE") with 9 pages (Login,
Dashboard, Customer List, Segmentation, Prediction, Analytics, Model
Comparison, Reports, Settings), KPI cards, Chart.js visualisations,
DataTables search/pagination, dark mode, and Excel/PDF export. Full
technical walkthrough in the README and inline code comments.

### 12. Limitations
- Cold-start: new customers with no order history can't yet be scored by
  the trained model (rule-based fallback only).
- The dataset's monetary/spend columns are proxies, not exact transaction
  ledgers — `EstimatedAnnualSpendINR` is a documented derived estimate,
  not raw currency data from Kaggle.
- Single-snapshot dataset — no true time-series validation of "did the
  predicted churners actually churn N months later."

### 13. Conclusion
The system demonstrates a complete, defensible pipeline — real data,
compared models, explainable outputs, and a working dashboard — that maps
directly onto a genuine Indian retail retention workflow rather than a
toy classroom exercise.
